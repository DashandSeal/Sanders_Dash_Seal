TRUST · IDENTITY VERIFICATION

Identity, Verified
Before the Act.

A notarization is only worth what the identity beneath it is worth. This page explains how Sanders Dash & Seal verifies identity — at intake, in person, and, for remote acts, through the credential analysis and proofing the platform is designed to perform before any session opens.

AT INTAKE

Verification Begins With the Request

Identity discipline starts before anyone meets.

Independent Verification

The booking request carries your consent to have its details verified independently. That consent is part of the form itself — verification is not an afterthought.

In Person

At a mobile appointment, the notary examines each signer's government-issued photo identification at the table, before the act. Expired or unverifiable identification stops the signing.

On the Record

The identification presented is carried in the journal record of the act — the evidence a future question will ask for.

FOR REMOTE ACTS

Credential Analysis & Proofing, As Designed

A remote signer cannot hand the notary a card. The platform's design answers with two independent checks, drawn from the verification sources the law requires.

Credential Analysis

The signer's government-issued credential is analyzed — its security features and data examined for authenticity — before the session proceeds.

Knowledge-Based Proofing

A second, independent proofing step confirms the person presenting the credential is the person it names, using legally required verification sources.

A Recorded Decision

Identity is one of the authority decisions the platform records for every remote act — alongside commission, jurisdiction, method, and human attestation. The decision stands in the record, or the act does not happen.

FAIL-CLOSED

The rule has no exceptions: if identity cannot be verified, the act does not proceed — in person or remote, at any price, on any deadline.
