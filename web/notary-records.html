WHAT IS KEPT

The Journal & the Evidence Record

Two records stand behind every engagement.

The Journal

Each notarial act is entered in the journal: the date, the parties, the act performed, and the identification presented. The journal is the notary's legal memory of the act.

The Evidence Record

The platform's evidence index carries the engagement's trail — request, review, quote, payment, and completion — each entry fingerprinted, so the record can be proven untampered.

Session Recordings

Remote sessions are recorded, and the platform's recording manifests carry a ten-year retention rule — the record of a remote act is kept to the standard its law expects.

HOW TO REQUEST

In Writing, Identity Verified

Records of a notarial act are not public documents, and they are not released on a phone call.

Request in Writing

A record request is made in writing through the client portal, identifying the act — its date, the parties, and the document involved.

Identity Verified First

The requester's identity and standing are verified before any record is released. A journal entry concerns its parties; it is released to those with the right to it, as the applicable law provides.

Answered from the Record

The answer is drawn from the journal and the evidence record as they stand. The record is never reconstructed from memory.

GLASS-BOX
