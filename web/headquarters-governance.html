Corporate Governance

How the house is governed — one owner, decisions made by name, and a signed record for every executive action.

The Sanders Companies are governed from their corporate headquarters, S & G Security Inc., under the sole ownership of their founder, Roosevelt Sanders. Each company of the house keeps its own legal identity, its own registry facts, and its own seal; governance — the standard, the approvals, and the accountability — is held at headquarters.

Receipts, Not Recollections

Executive actions in the house are witnessed by Bozly, the headquarters integrity office: authorized actions carry cryptographic receipts proving which authorized officer signed them, and documented claims are checked against the recorded transaction before they are answered. Bozly holds no power to move money — its office is integrity, not spending. Under the house's Glass-Box discipline, there is no record without an action, and no action without a record.

The house's governance framework — its charter documents, bylaws framework, and the founder's rulings — is kept in the platform's conformance record, where directives are entered as the founder issues them and verified as they land. Governance documents of the house include the Permanent Bloodline Protection Directive, whose public statement stands on this website.

THE FRAMEWORK

How Governance Stands

One Owner

The founder is the sole owner and the only approver of the house; succession is governed by the Permanent Bloodline Protection Directive.

Named Approvals

Material decisions are approved by name, per item — the house does not run on blanket approvals.

Signed Receipts

Executive actions carry Bozly's cryptographic receipts; claims are checked against the recorded transaction.

A Kept Record

Directives and rulings are entered into the conformance record as issued, and verified byte for byte as they land.
