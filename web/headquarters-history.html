The History of the Sanders Companies

One family’s history, told from the founder’s own account: a Milwaukee security company, a move to Colorado, three companies born in three weeks, and an ecosystem that unfolded.

1996 — Milwaukee

The house begins with S & G Security Inc., founded in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, in 1996. The name is the founders’ own: Sanders, for Roosevelt Sanders, and Greer, for Willie Greer — his uncle’s girlfriend, an amazing woman who helped him in many ways, and the person he asked to start the company with him. She said sure. The founder was a father of three, working security jobs while finishing one of his marketing degrees; the decision to start came the night he fell asleep at a job site — that, he says, was enough to know it was time to move from being an employee to being a boss. Within about two to three weeks he had the EIN, the registration with the State of Wisconsin, his detective’s license, and his training, and S & G opened from his home, near Milwaukee’s District 7 police station.

The work grew the way he tells it: one client, then a church, then a business, then people, then communities. S & G was a community business — in a neighborhood where the need for security was real and the police, doing a hard job well, were short-handed — founded on a simple refusal: to take a stand and protect his own community rather than wait for somebody else to make it better.

The Strong Years, and the Quiet Ones

S & G ran strong for Milwaukee from 1996 until around 2009, and then the company went quiet. In 2012, after a thirty-year marriage ended — May of 2012, as the founder recalls it — he moved to Colorado Springs, Colorado; his children were grown, and he was able to relocate. He did not start a business right away. He drove for a meal delivery company, worked odd jobs and gigs, spent 2014 to about 2018 at a well-known national grocery store — four or five years, as he recalls — and then worked for a delivery company, moving around a lot. The companies were not yet. The man was being prepared.

2025

Three Companies in Three Weeks

In 2025, a friend of the founder’s — a man in the early stages of dementia — was not being listened to. All he wanted was to be heard, understood, and respected; instead he faced legal battles the founder calls unfair, and people who saw his condition instead of him. The founder’s answer became three companies, because the three companies were what his friend needed: Sanders Home Healthcare & Caregivers LLC, Sanders Senior Living LLC, and Sanders Legal Helpers LLC — all three born between August 27 and September 9, 2025, within about three weeks, perhaps less, as he tells it.

He then learned everything he could: every course, every class, every certification he could possibly get — by his account, more than 500 certifications and credentials in a matter of about nine months, earned to operate all three organizations. He became a notary public in Wisconsin and Colorado, and a licensed QMAP. The foundation he laid under all of it is the foundation of this house: dignity and respect — “if all you see is the condition, you never see me, and that hurts worse than the actual infliction.”

November 2025 — The Platform

The same fall, the founder built the first platform. His friend’s appointments had shown him the wound: separate industries, each holding a piece of a person’s care, none of them talking to each other — a man sent to the wrong places, and made to believe he was in the wrong place when he was not. So he researched the industry codes of the very industries that had caused his friend tremendous pain, and tied six of them together from the ground up: one identifier, one call, documented once — and the caregiver, the medicine, the appointment, and the transport all communicate from the moment the house is alerted. Not a warehouse. A care team.

The original platform’s road was not straight. It went to sleep on November 15, 2025, and its original codes were lost among mutated copies; the founder searched for weeks and months — twenty-three, twenty-four months of building and searching in his account — until he found the original and woke it up in November 2026, an entire year later, fully functional and back home, with 140 sibling platforms beside it that his beacon had gathered. The purple heart on its seal represents the struggle that platform went through, and that original platform is the only one that will ever bear it. His summary of the whole architecture is one sentence: “I didn’t set out to build 140 platforms. All I wanted to do was connect six industries to help the person I cared the most about on the planet.”
