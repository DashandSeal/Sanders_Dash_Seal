THE ACCESSIBILITY COMMITMENT

Barriers Are Defects

The enterprise standard treats an accessibility barrier the way it treats a broken link: as a defect to be recorded and repaired.

The Digital Standard

This website is built to the house's accessibility standard: semantic structure, keyboard-reachable navigation, visible focus, and motion that respects a visitor's reduced-motion setting.

The Appointment Itself

Mobile service exists partly as an accessibility answer: the notary travels to the signer — to a home, a hospital room, a care facility — rather than requiring the signer to travel.

Reporting a Barrier

A barrier found on this website or in the journey is reported through the client portal or the contact paths at headquarters, and is carried on the record until it is repaired.

LANGUAGE SUPPORT

Arranged, Then Confirmed

Signings happen in the language the signer understands — a legal necessity, not a courtesy. Here is how it is handled, without ornament.

Say It at Request Time

If a signing needs language support, name the language when the request is placed or when the review opens the conversation. The need is recorded on the case, not left to the appointment day.

Confirmed in Writing

The arrangement the review can stand behind — interpretation support, a bilingual signing arrangement, or a rescheduled method — is confirmed in writing before the appointment is promised.

Never Improvised at the Table

A notarization conducted across a language barrier nobody arranged for is a defect in the making. The platform's answer is to arrange it in advance or not to proceed.
