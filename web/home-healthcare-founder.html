SANDERS HOME HEALTHCARE & CAREGIVERS LLC

The Founder

Roosevelt Sanders — founder and owner. This company exists because a person he cared about needed to be heard, and nobody would listen. He listened — and then he built the company that listens.

Roosevelt Sanders founded Sanders Home Healthcare & Caregivers LLC in 2025, in the same three weeks that brought its sister companies into being — born, all three of them, out of one man’s need: a friend in the early stages of dementia who wanted only to be heard, understood, and respected, and who was not being heard. The founder’s response was to learn. He took every course, every class, every certification he could get — by his account, more than 500 certifications and credentials in a matter of about nine months, earned to operate these companies properly. He is a licensed QMAP, and a notary public in Wisconsin and Colorado.

Care With a Family Name on It

The founder is not a distant owner of this company; its standard is his daily conduct. Care here is delivered by independent contractors of the caregiver network, working to a written care plan, with every visit checked in and recorded — because a family should never have to wonder, and a person should never be reduced to a condition.

THE FOUNDER’S STATEMENT

“Everybody deserves dignity and respect,” the founder says. “It doesn’t matter what you have. A condition doesn’t care if you’re rich or poor — an affliction doesn’t care. What matters is how you are treated, each and every day.”

Disrespect, in this house, is decided by the person who feels disrespected. If you feel disrespected, no one here will tell you that you were not, and no one will explain your feelings away. That is why this house treats a person — never a condition. The condition is not the person.

And it is why the person themselves decides. The founder will not accept a resident or a client who is not themselves at peace with being accepted; a family member’s assurance is not an answer, and a power of attorney does not override the person’s own refusal or discomfort. “Give it a couple of days and bring them back. I’m not going anywhere. These doors are not going to close because they didn’t walk through them.” Readiness is the person’s to decide — all we can do is wait. “That’s integrity. That’s dignity. That’s respect in action.”
