Bloodline Succession & Legacy

The public statement of the Permanent Bloodline Protection Directive — the founder's permanent mandate for the house he built.

On November 25, 2026, Roosevelt Sanders adopted the Permanent Bloodline Protection Directive as his formal last wishes for the Sanders Companies — his dying declaration for the house, entered into the governance record of the platform the same day. This page is the Directive's public statement; the instrument itself is a governance record of the house, held with the founder's estate documents.

What the Directive Holds

The Directive protects the birthright the founder is building for his bloodline, permanently. Designated successors are named in the Directive itself. No successor, and no generation after them, may disinherit, exclude, or transfer that birthright outside the bloodline; succession in leadership must never become a mechanism for destroying inheritance. After the founder's lifetime, the Directive may only be strengthened — never weakened, rewritten, or diluted.

The companies and platforms of the house stand under the same protection: they are never on the table, never sold, and never converted into anyone's bargaining piece. The birthright the Directive protects is inheritance and standing in the founder's legacy — the house itself remains the house, governed from its headquarters under the standard the founder set.

THE MANDATE

The Directive, in Brief

Permanent

Adopted November 25, 2026, and entered into the platform's governance record — it does not expire and cannot lapse.

Strengthen-Only

After the founder's lifetime the Directive may be strengthened; it may never be weakened or narrowed.

No Disinheritance

No bloodline relative may be disinherited or excluded, and none may disown another, under the Directive's terms.

The House Stands

The companies and platforms are never sold and never on the table — in any generation, under any circumstance.
