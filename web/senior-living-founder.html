SANDERS SENIOR LIVING LLC

The Founder

Roosevelt Sanders — founder and owner. This company was born because a man deserved to be heard in his own life, and the founder refused to build him a warehouse.

Roosevelt Sanders founded Sanders Senior Living LLC in 2025, in the same three weeks that brought its sister companies into being — born out of one man’s need: a friend in the early stages of dementia who wanted only to be heard, understood, and respected. The founder’s standard for this house was set by that friendship: a residence is a person’s home, and the person living in it is not a condition to be managed but a life to be honored.

A House, Not a Warehouse

The founder prepared himself the way he prepares for everything: he learned. By his account he earned more than 500 certifications and credentials in a matter of about nine months, to operate these companies properly — and he built this company into a platform where, from the moment the house is alerted to a need, the whole care team can communicate: the caregiver, the medicine, the appointment, the transport. That is what makes an effective care team.

THE FOUNDER’S STATEMENT

“Everybody deserves dignity and respect,” the founder says. “It doesn’t matter what you have. A condition doesn’t care if you’re rich or poor — an affliction doesn’t care. What matters is how you are treated, each and every day.”

Disrespect, in this house, is decided by the person who feels disrespected. If you feel disrespected, no one here will tell you that you were not, and no one will explain your feelings away. That is why this house treats a person — never a condition. The condition is not the person.

And it is why the person themselves decides. The founder will not accept a resident or a client who is not themselves at peace with being accepted; a family member’s assurance is not an answer, and a power of attorney does not override the person’s own refusal or discomfort. “Give it a couple of days and bring them back. I’m not going anywhere. These doors are not going to close because they didn’t walk through them.” Readiness is the person’s to decide — all we can do is wait. “That’s integrity. That’s dignity. That’s respect in action.”
