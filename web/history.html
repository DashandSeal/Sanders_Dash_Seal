THE NEED

He Didn’t Know Where to Go

In 2025, the founder’s friend — a man in the early stages of dementia — had a great many things that needed to be notarized, and a great many things that needed preparing. It was not that he couldn’t understand how to do them. It was that he didn’t know where to go to get the information. What he did have was a phone, and he was always on it, doing research.

The founder did two things. The first was to turn his friend’s condition into his strength: if all you’re doing is looking things up — look up how to write this, how to write that, how to prepare your documents for court. His friend’s face lit up. What he found and discovered led him to write his own power of attorney — a thing he would never ordinarily have thought to do for himself.

THE COURTROOM

No One Heard Him — So I Became a Notary

They went to court with documents the friend had prepared himself. And his family said he couldn’t do it — because of his condition. That is exactly what they blamed it on: his condition. They didn’t know the man the founder knew. The friend told them that Roosevelt Sanders was the only person he trusted — and that they were trying to take the only person he trusted away. No one heard him.

“So I became a notary.” And here is the part the founder calls the beautiful part: his friend never once asked him to violate his notaryship, and never asked him to put his name to anything that needed signing. He simply knew the founder would take him to get it notarized — and the founder did. Every appointment.

ONE PLACE TO GO

The Platform

The same friend’s appointments had shown the founder the larger wound: industries that each held a piece of a person’s life and did not talk to each other — a man told to go to the wrong place, and made to believe he was in the wrong place when he was not. So the founder researched the industry codes of the very industries that had caused his friend tremendous pain, and tied six of them together from the ground up: one identifier, one place to go, and everything sets up and falls into place.

The original platform went to sleep on November 15, 2025, and woke in November 2026, an entire year later, with 140 sibling platforms beside it. The purple heart on its corporate seal represents the struggle that platform went through — and that original platform is the only one that will ever bear it. The founder’s summary stands over this whole website: “I didn’t set out to build 140 platforms. All I wanted to do was connect six industries to help the person I cared the most about on the planet.”
