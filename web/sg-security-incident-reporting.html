Incident Reporting

How an incident gets on the record — by the officer who stood there, and by the client who needs it answered.

In this house, an incident that is not recorded did not get answered. Incident reporting is the discipline that makes the rest of the service real: every incident on an S & G post is written as it happens — the time, the place, who was present, what was observed, what was done — and entered into the engagement's record, where the client can read it and where any later claim about it is answered from it.

Officers report as a duty of the post: no incident is too small for the log, because the log is what makes the small ones provable and the large ones answerable. Reports are factual by rule — observations, not conclusions; times, not estimates — and they are made before the shift that saw the incident closes.

Clients report through the same record: through the client portal under their sign-in, or through this website's contact paths when the matter cannot wait for a sign-in. A client report should carry the same furniture as an officer's — when, where, who, what — and it enters the same record, where it is answered against the recorded engagement, not against recollection. In an emergency in progress, the first call is always to emergency services; this page governs the record, never the response to a live danger.
